## Recommended outline

### 1. Why accurate inputs change a freight quote

- Explain actual versus estimated weight and dimensions.
- Explain that origin, destination, delivery-site access, cargo characteristics, and service scope affect feasibility and price.
- Avoid generic claims about the cheapest or fastest method.

### 2. Product and commercial information

Checklist:

- accurate product name, intended use, photos, materials, and model;
- quantity and sales unit;
- cargo value, currency, and trade-document status;
- buyer, seller, shipper, consignee, importer, and delivery recipient where known;
- known product-compliance or destination-market concerns.

Do not provide tariff classification, customs, tax, or legal advice.

### 3. Packing, dimensions, and weight

Checklist:

- unit packing;
- number of cartons, crates, or pallets;
- packed length, width, and height for each package type;
- gross weight for each package type and total;
- palletization, stackability, orientation, fragility, and handling constraints;
- photos of actual or proposed packing.

Do not publish a universal oversized or overweight threshold.

### 4. Origin, ready date, and supplier handoffs

Checklist:

- supplier or pickup city;
- number of suppliers;
- goods-ready date for each supplier;
- whether cargo needs receiving, matching, labeling, repacking, storage, or consolidation;
- export-document readiness and responsible party.

Link to `China sourcing and supplier coordination` and the multi-supplier consolidation section on the freight page.

### 5. Destination and final delivery

Checklist:

- destination country, city, and postal code;
- exact named delivery place or address;
- commercial or residential location;
- appointment, dock, forklift, liftgate, inside-delivery, access, or restricted-hour requirements;
- importer and broker arrangement;
- target delivery requirement without presenting it as a guaranteed date.

### 6. Declare special cargo before route review

Cover:

- batteries and battery-containing equipment;
- liquids, powders, magnets, chemicals, brands, or regulated goods;
- dangerous-goods status if known;
- oversized, overweight, fragile, or high-value cargo;
- all available compliance and transport documents.

State the verified exclusion boundary: no prohibited goods, restricted imports or exports, counterfeit or infringing goods, or cargo without required compliance documents.

Do not publish a universal battery-document checklist. Required documents and acceptance depend on the actual product, packing, origin, destination, carrier, and route.

### 7. Service term and quotation boundaries

Explain that DDP and DAP are not complete price descriptions by themselves. Ask the quotation to mark relevant items as included, excluded, not applicable, or pending confirmation, including:

- pickup and export handling;
- main transport;
- customs brokerage;
- duties and VAT;
- destination handling;
- remote-area, storage, re-delivery, appointment, and special-equipment charges;
- insurance and claim handling;
- unloading and final-site access.

Link to `DDP Shipping From China` and `DDP vs DAP Shipping From China`.

### 8. How to compare two freight quotations

Recommend a side-by-side comparison of:

- product and packed-data assumptions;
- transport method and route;
- chargeable basis;
- named place and final-delivery scope;
- customs and importer responsibilities;
- included and excluded charges;
- validity, adjustment triggers, and exception handling.

Do not publish example rates or fabricated quotations.
